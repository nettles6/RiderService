How to Change Motorcycle Oil: Step by Step

An oil and filter change is one of the most rewarding jobs you can do at home, and it is the service that matters most to engine life. This is a general overview. Always follow your manual for oil grade, quantity, filter type and torque figures.

What you need

  • The correct oil grade and quantity from your manual
  • A new oil filter that fits your bike
  • A new sump plug washer (crush washer), if your bike uses one
  • Drain pan, funnel and rags
  • Spanner or socket for the sump plug and a filter tool that suits your filter
  • A torque wrench
  • A paddock stand or centre stand so the bike sits upright and stable
  • Gloves and eye protection

The steps

  1. Warm the engine briefly. A few minutes of running thins the oil so it drains well. Do not drain a fully hot engine, as the oil and exhaust can burn.
  2. Position the bike upright and level, with the drain pan under the sump plug.
  3. Remove the filler cap, then the sump plug, and let the oil drain completely.
  4. Remove the old filter over the pan, as it will hold oil. Check the old filter's seal came off with it.
  5. Fit the new filter. Wipe the mating surface clean and lightly oil the new seal. Tighten to the torque in your manual.
  6. Refit the sump plug with a new washer and tighten to the specified torque.
  7. Add fresh oil to the amount in your manual, then check the level using the sight glass or dipstick, following your bike's procedure.
  8. Run the engine briefly, check for leaks around the filter and sump plug, then recheck the oil level after it settles.

Common mistakes

  • Overtightening the sump plug or filter, which can strip threads or damage seals
  • Reusing the old crush washer
  • Overfilling or underfilling, which are both harmful
  • Leaving the old filter seal stuck on the engine
  • Using car oil in a bike with a wet clutch. Many motorcycles need oil that meets a motorcycle-specific specification, so check the manual and the bottle

Disposing of used oil

Never pour oil down a drain or into the ground. Take it in a sealed container to a council waste facility or a recycling point that accepts used oil. Many auto parts stores also accept it.
