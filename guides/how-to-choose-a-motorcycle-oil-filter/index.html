How to Choose a Motorcycle Oil Filter

The oil filter removes metal particles, carbon and dirt from the oil before it circulates through your engine. A filter that does not fit, or that is poorly made, can leak or let contaminants through. A few checks make the choice simple.

Spin-on and cartridge filters

Motorcycle oil filters come in two main designs, and your engine is built for one or the other.

  • Spin-on filters are sealed metal canisters that screw onto the engine. You remove the whole unit and fit a new one.
  • Cartridge filters are a paper or media element that sits inside a housing in the engine. You replace the element and usually the housing's O-rings or seals.

You cannot swap one type for the other. To find the oil filters that fit your bike you can use our search function. It is here to help you find the filters specific to your bike.

OEM or aftermarket

Genuine manufacturer filters are the safe default. Quality aftermarket brands are widely used and often cost less, and many are made to the same fit and performance. The main risk is very cheap, unbranded filters, where fit and construction can be inconsistent. Choose a recognised brand and confirm it is listed for your exact bike.

Replace it every oil change

Some manufacturers will list an oil change without replacing the oil filter. We recommend you fit a new oil filter each time you change the oil. Running fresh oil into a used filter increases the risk of a clogged filter before the next service. Lightly oil the new gasket or O-ring before fitting. Overtightening can damage the seal or housing.
