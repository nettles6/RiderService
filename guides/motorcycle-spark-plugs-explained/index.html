Motorcycle Spark Plugs Explained

A spark plug ignites the fuel and air mixture in each cylinder. Worn or incorrect plugs cause hard starting, misfires, poor fuel economy and rough running. Choosing the right plug comes down to a few specifications.

Common plug types

  • Copper (nickel alloy): inexpensive with good conductivity, but the electrode wears faster, so it needs replacing sooner.
  • Platinum: a harder electrode that lasts longer than copper.
  • Iridium: a very hard, fine-wire electrode that typically lasts the longest and holds a consistent spark.

Follow the type your manufacturer specifies. A longer-lasting plug is not automatically a better fit for every engine.

Heat range

The heat range describes how quickly a plug sheds heat from its tip. A plug that runs too cold can foul with deposits. One that runs too hot can overheat and risk pre-ignition or engine damage. Your manual specifies the correct heat range, shown as a number or letter code in the plug's part number. Brands use different scales, so a higher number does not mean the same thing across brands.

Thread, reach and gap

  • Thread diameter and reach: the plug must match the cylinder head. A plug that is too short or too long is unsafe.
  • Gap: the distance between the electrodes. Check it with a feeler gauge against the manual's figure, even on new plugs. Do not force a gap open by levering on the centre electrode, particularly on fine-wire iridium plugs.

Signs a plug needs replacing

  • Hard starting or a weak, uneven idle
  • Misfires or hesitation under load
  • Reduced fuel economy or power
  • Worn, rounded electrodes or heavy deposits on the tip

The colour and condition of a used plug can also hint at how the engine is running, but a persistent problem is best looked at by a mechanic.

Fitting tips

Fit plugs into a cool engine, start them by hand to avoid cross-threading and tighten to the specified torque. Many bikes have deep, tight plug wells, so the correct plug socket is a worthwhile purchase. Use the parts finder on our home page to see the plugs listed for your bike.
