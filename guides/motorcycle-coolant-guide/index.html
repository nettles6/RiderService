Motorcycle Coolant: Checking, Topping Up and Replacing

On a liquid-cooled motorcycle, coolant carries heat from the engine to the radiator. It also protects the system against freezing and corrosion. Low or tired coolant is one of the easier causes of overheating to prevent.

Does your bike need it?

Many motorcycles are air-cooled or air-and-oil-cooled and have no coolant at all. If your bike has a radiator and an overflow bottle, it is liquid-cooled and this guide applies.

Check the level regularly

Check the coolant level with the bike upright and the engine cold. Most bikes have a translucent overflow bottle with minimum and maximum marks. The level should sit between them. Looking at it takes a few seconds, so make it part of a regular pre-ride routine.

Never open the radiator cap on a hot engine. The system is pressurised and can spray scalding coolant.

Choosing the right coolant

  • Use the coolant type and mixture ratio your manual specifies. Many bikes use an ethylene glycol coolant, often with corrosion inhibitors suited to aluminium engines.
  • Do not mix different coolant types or colours unless the maker says they are compatible.
  • If you use concentrate, dilute it with distilled or deionised water, not tap water, which can leave deposits.
  • Pre-mixed coolant is the simplest option for topping up.

Signs of a cooling problem

  • The temperature gauge or warning light showing higher than normal
  • A coolant level that keeps dropping
  • Puddles under the bike, or a sweet smell after a ride
  • Steam from the radiator or overflow
  • A radiator fan that never switches on, or runs constantly

If the bike overheats, pull over safely and switch off the engine. A recurring drop in level points to a leak that should be traced, not just topped up.

Replacing coolant

Coolant breaks down over time, so manufacturers set a time and distance interval for replacing it. The job involves draining the system, flushing if recommended and refilling, then bleeding any trapped air so the engine does not run hot. Hoses and the radiator cap are worth checking at the same time.

Disposal

Coolant is toxic and tastes sweet, which attracts animals. Clean up spills at once, store it in a sealed, labelled container, and take used coolant to a council waste facility or recycling point.
