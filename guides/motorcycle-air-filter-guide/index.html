Motorcycle Air Filters: Types, Cleaning and Replacement

Your engine needs a large volume of clean air to burn fuel properly. The air filter stops dust, grit and insects from entering the cylinders, where they cause rapid wear. It is a cheap part that protects an expensive one.

Common air filter types

  • Paper elements are the most common on road bikes. They are replaced when dirty rather than cleaned.
  • Foam elements are common on off-road and some road bikes. They can be washed, dried and re-oiled with a filter oil made for the job.
  • Oiled cotton gauze elements are a reusable aftermarket design, cleaned and re-oiled with a matching kit.

Use the type your bike is designed for, and follow the cleaning method the filter's maker specifies.

How often to check it

Your manual lists an inspection and replacement interval, but the real answer depends on where you ride. Dusty roads, unsealed tracks, heavy traffic and wet conditions all load the filter faster. Look at it at every oil change, and more often if you ride off-road.

Signs of a dirty or failing filter

  • Visible dirt, oil contamination or a blocked, matted surface
  • Reduced power or a flat feeling when you accelerate
  • Poorer fuel economy
  • Rough running or hesitation
  • Tears, holes or gaps around the sealing edge

These symptoms can have other causes too, so use them as a prompt to inspect the filter, not as a diagnosis.

Cleaning and replacing

If you have a washable filter, clean it with the correct solvent or cleaner, let it dry fully, then apply the right amount of filter oil. Too little oil lets dirt through, and too much can restrict airflow. A paper filter that is dirty or damaged should be replaced. Check that the airbox seals properly when you refit the lid, because dirt that bypasses the filter defeats its purpose.

Never run without one

Even a short ride without an air filter lets abrasive particles into the engine. A fuel-injected bike may also run poorly without the airflow it expects. If you are mid-job and the filter is out, keep the airbox covered.
