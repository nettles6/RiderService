Motorcycle Chain Maintenance: Clean, Lube and Adjust

The drive chain carries all of your engine's power to the rear wheel. A well-kept chain can last a long time, while a neglected one wears quickly, wears out the sprockets with it and can fail at the worst moment.

Know your chain

Most modern road bike chains are sealed with O-rings or X-rings that hold grease inside the links. They need cleaning and lubricating, but they are sensitive to the wrong products. Use a cleaner and lubricant that the chain maker says are safe for sealed chains. Some bikes use a shaft or belt drive instead, which has completely different maintenance, so check your manual.

Cleaning the chain

  1. Put the bike on a stand so the rear wheel turns freely.
  2. Apply a chain-safe cleaner and work it in with a soft chain brush.
  3. Rotate the wheel to reach the whole length, then wipe away the grime with a rag.
  4. Let the chain dry before lubricating.

Avoid harsh solvents and high-pressure water, which can damage seals or force dirt in.

Lubricating the chain

Apply lubricant to the inside run of the chain, where it spreads outwards as the chain moves. Do it after a ride, when the chain is slightly warm, and give it time to settle before the next ride. Wipe off any excess, as too much lube flings onto the rear wheel and attracts dirt. Lubricate regularly, and more often after wet rides or long trips.

Checking and adjusting slack

Check slack with the bike on a stand, at several points around the chain, as chains wear unevenly. Measure at the tightest point and compare it with the figure in your manual. A chain that is too loose can jump off the sprocket. One that is too tight overloads the gearbox and wheel bearings.

After adjusting, make sure the rear wheel is aligned correctly using the marks or method in your manual, and tighten the axle to its specified torque.

Signs of wear

  • Stiff or kinked links that do not flex freely
  • Rust that stays after cleaning
  • Slack that keeps growing and cannot be adjusted out
  • Sprocket teeth that look hooked, shark-finned or thin
  • Excess chain stretch compared with the limit in your manual

Replace the set together

A worn chain wears the sprockets, and worn sprockets quickly wear a new chain. For that reason, a chain and both sprockets are normally replaced as a set. Choose a chain with the right pitch, size and length for your bike. You can check what is listed for it using the parts finder on our home page.
