How to Read Your Motorcycle's Service Schedule

The service schedule in your owner's manual is a dense table of items, symbols and numbers. Once you know how to read it, you can see exactly what your bike needs at each stage and what is worth doing yourself.

Find the table

Look in the owner's manual for a section named something like periodic maintenance or maintenance schedule. Some manufacturers publish a separate service manual with more detail, including torque figures and procedures. A bike's logbook or service booklet usually repeats the schedule and has pages for stamps or notes.

Typical symbols

Manufacturers use their own codes, so check the key printed with the table. Common ones include:

  • I for inspect, and then clean, adjust, lubricate or replace as needed
  • R for replace
  • L for lubricate
  • C for clean
  • A for adjust
  • T for tighten to the specified torque

Distance and time

Intervals are usually listed by odometer reading and by time. Service when you reach whichever comes first. A low-mileage bike still ages, so oil, brake fluid and coolant are due on the calendar even if the odometer has barely moved. Some items are only inspected on a schedule and replaced when found worn.

The first service

New bikes typically have an early service after a short running-in period, where the oil and filter are changed and fasteners and settings are checked. It is often the most important service of the bike's life, so do not skip it, and check how it affects your warranty terms.

Severe use changes the schedule

Many manuals note that dust, short trips, heavy loads, hot weather or aggressive riding call for shorter intervals. If that sounds like your riding, treat the table as the longest you should wait, not the target.

Make it work for you

  • Copy the table into a checklist or spreadsheet for your own bike.
  • Mark which jobs you can do yourself and which you will book into a workshop.
  • Record the date, odometer and work done after every service.
  • Use the parts finder on our home page to line up the filters and plugs for each service in advance.
